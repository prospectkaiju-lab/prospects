Where to Stay

Two worlds, one gondola

Telluride offers a genuine choice between two distinct experiences: the historic Victorian town at the canyon floor, or the purpose-built Mountain Village at elevation — connected by a free 13-minute gondola that runs until midnight.

Auberge Resorts · Top Pick
Madeline Hotel & Residences
Telluride's finest hotel — 96 luxurious rooms and 29 condos in Mountain Village, ski-in/ski-out access, the Black Iron Kitchen + Bar, Timber Room dining, a 4,000-square-foot spa, and a Sky Terrace with heated pool and fire pits. The definitive Telluride stay.

Historic · Best in Town
New Sheridan Hotel

Restaurants & Dining

Dining with altitude

Telluride's restaurant scene is remarkable for a town of 2,500 permanent residents — and firmly committed to having no chain restaurants. Everything here is independent, local, and usually excellent.

The Madeline's signature cocktail bar and dinner restaurant — outdoor fire tables, an indoor/outdoor bar, and modern mountain cuisine with the San Juan peaks as backdrop. The best atmosphere for après-ski dinner in Telluride.

Things to Do

The canyon, yours to discover

Telluride's terrain is unlike anything else in Colorado — dramatic, challenging, and set in a box canyon that makes every run feel like the end of the world in the best possible way.

01
Ski Plunge or Spiral Stairs — Extreme Faces
Telluride's signature black diamond runs drop directly into the canyon from 12,000 feet — some of the steepest lift-served terrain in North America. Plunge and Spiral Stairs are the reasons serious skiers make this pilgrimage.
⛷️ Expert Only
02
Ride the Free Gondola Between Two Worlds
The Telluride Gondola connects the historic town to Mountain Village in 13 minutes — free, runs until midnight, and offers some of the most extraordinary views of the San Juan Mountains in existence. Ride it at sunset specifically. Don't skip this.
🚡 Free · Daily
03
Walk the Historic District of Colorado Avenue
Four blocks of Victorian storefronts unchanged since the silver mining era — independent shops, galleries, and restaurants with not a chain in sight. Walk the full length, duck into everything, and end at the New Sheridan Bar. The best preserved Victorian main street in Colorado.
🏘️ Cultural Experience
04
Bridal Veil Falls — Summer Hike
Colorado's longest free-falling waterfall drops 365 feet at the east end of the box canyon — visible from town and hikeable in summer via a stunning valley trail. The historic power plant at the top is still operational.
🏞️ Summer / Fall
05
Telluride Film Festival
Every Labor Day weekend, one of the world's most prestigious film festivals takes over the town — world premieres, outdoor screenings under the stars, and an audience of filmmakers and cinephiles. More intimate than Sundance and uniquely Telluride.
🎬 Labor Day Weekend
06
Skinning Up the Backcountry
The terrain around Telluride opens into serious backcountry skiing — guided tours lead experienced skiers into the San Juan wilderness beyond the resort boundary. The scenery alone justifies the effort. Guides required; conditions variable.
🎿 Guided Tours Available
Getting There

Finding your way to the canyon

Telluride's remoteness is part of its magic — and its main logistical challenge. The regional airport is dramatic; the drive from Denver takes seven hours. Plan carefully.

✈️
Telluride Regional Airport (TEX)
One of the highest commercial airports in North America — dramatic approach, limited flights, and severe weather sensitivity. United flies from Denver seasonally. The approach into TEX is one of aviation's great experiences. Weather cancellations are common.
Always have a Montrose backup plan. TEX closures without warning are part of the Telluride experience.
✈️
Montrose Regional (MTJ) — Primary Option
The practical choice — 65 miles from Telluride (about 70 minutes by shuttle), served by American, United, Delta, and Southwest with solid connectivity from major hubs. More reliable than TEX and where most smart travelers arrive.
I recommend MTJ for most clients — the reliability and connectivity make it the sensible default. The drive through the canyon is beautiful.
🚐
Shuttle from Montrose
Several shuttle operators run scheduled service from Montrose to Telluride and Mountain Village — about 70 minutes, ~$60 per person each way. Book in advance for peak weeks. The Madeline and most Mountain Village hotels also offer private transfer arrangements.
I coordinate airport-to-hotel transfers for my Telluride clients — one less moving piece in a complicated arrival.
🚗
Drive from Denver
Seven hours via US-50 through the Gunnison and Cimarron valleys — one of the most stunningly beautiful drives in Colorado, through Black Canyon country. 4WD with snow tires required in winter. An adventure in itself, best with an early start.
The drive from Denver is extraordinary but long. Consider flying in and renting from Montrose for maximum efficiency.
✦
The Free Gondola — Your Best Friend
Once you're in Telluride, the free gondola between town and Mountain Village runs continuously until midnight — making a car completely unnecessary during your stay. This is one of the great amenities in American ski resort design.

Advisor's Telluride tips
  • Fly into Montrose (MTJ) for reliability, even though the TEX approach is extraordinary. Weather at TEX can close the airport without warning.
  • Town vs. Mountain Village is the central decision. Town for atmosphere and history; Mountain Village for ski-in/ski-out convenience.
  • Altitude in Telluride hits harder than Aspen — you're at 8,750 feet in town, 12,000+ on the mountain. Acclimatize your first day.
  • The gondola runs until midnight — use it to move freely between the two sides of the mountain without a car.
  • Book Allred's early — there are only so many gondola dinner tables at 10,551 feet.
  • Telluride has a genuine no-chain-restaurant commitment. Everything you eat will be independent and locally owned.
Best times to visit
  • January – February — Deep powder, full mountain, most dramatic skiing
  • Late March — Spring skiing, sunshine, lower crowds and prices
  • Labor Day (Film Festival) — Cultural high point of the summer calendar
  • Avoid: Christmas week — highest prices and crowds of the season
