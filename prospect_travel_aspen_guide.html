Where to Stay

Mountain stays worth every dollar

Aspen's hotels are exceptional — and genuinely expensive. The decision between ski-in/ski-out, downtown access, and intimate vs. resort scale shapes everything about your trip.

Forbes 5-Star · Best in Class
The Little Nell
Aspen's only Forbes Five-Star, Five-Diamond hotel — at the base of Aspen Mountain, ski-in/ski-out, with 20,000-bottle wine cellar, Element 47 restaurant, and Ajax Tavern for lunch on the mountain. The unquestioned top of Aspen.
Ski-In/Ski-OutElement 47Forbes 5-StarWine CellarFora Preferred
✦The ski valet at The Little Nell is extraordinary — your skis are warm, dry, and ready each morning. Lunch at Ajax Tavern mid-ski-day is the quintessential Aspen experience.
Historic · Most Character
Hotel Jerome
Open since 1889 and the soul of Aspen — dark Victorian interiors, the legendary J-Bar, and a five-minute walk to the Silver Queen Gondola. Hunter S. Thompson drank here. Every inch of it feels like history.
Historic 1889J-BarAuberge CollectionDowntown
✦The J-Bar at Hotel Jerome is where Aspen happens after ski day. Arrive early for a barstool, order the house burger, and watch the town walk in. Don't skip this regardless of where you're staying.
Luxury · Best Service
St. Regis Aspen Resort
The largest hotel in Aspen — 179 rooms, Butler Service, the Remède Spa, and a sunset champagne saber tradition at the Mountain Social Bar. Ralph Lauren furnishings, heated marble floors, and 24-hour attention to every detail.
Butler ServiceRemède SpaMountain Social Bar179 Rooms
✦The St. Regis saber-and-champagne ceremony at the Mountain Social at sunset is one of the great Aspen rituals. Make sure you're there for it at least once.
Design · Most Distinctive
Aspen Meadows Resort
A Bauhaus masterpiece designed by Herbert Bayer — 40 serene acres, floor-to-ceiling windows, and a midcentury minimalism unlike anything else in Aspen. The intellectual's mountain retreat, home to the Aspen Institute.
Bauhaus Design40 AcresAspen InstituteMost Peaceful
✦For guests who want a quieter Aspen — the meadows setting, the architecture, and the proximity to summer cultural programming make this a genuine alternative to the more social hotels.
Restaurants & Dining

Après and beyond

Aspen's dining scene is serious — a resort town with the food culture of a real city. The challenge isn't finding good restaurants; it's choosing between them.

Element 47
$$$$
The Little Nell · Base of Aspen Mountain
The finest dining in Aspen — an extraordinary wine list (the name refers to silver's atomic number), impeccable mountain-contemporary cuisine, and service that earns The Little Nell its five stars. The tasting menu is the move.
→ Order: The tasting menu, anything from the wine list the sommelier recommends
Ajax Tavern
$$$
The Little Nell · Ski Base
The best lunch in mountain skiing — a lively terrace at the base of Aspen Mountain where skiers pull off the slopes and into heated outdoor seating. Truffle fries, excellent burgers, and a scene that only happens in Aspen.
→ Order: Ajax truffle fries, the Nell burger, a glass of something Burgundian
Jimmy's Bodega
$$
East Hopkins Avenue · Downtown
The most beloved casual spot in town — burritos, nachos, and margaritas in a lively, no-reservations setting that the whole valley ends up in eventually. The antidote to all the fine dining.

East Hopkins Avenue · Downtown
Nobu Matsuhisa's Aspen outpost — the original Matsuhisa, actually, which predates the Nobu brand. Impeccable omakase, black cod miso, and the best sake list in Colorado. Reserve weeks ahead.

Things to Do

Four mountains, one town

Aspen is genuinely four ski areas in one — Aspen Mountain, Aspen Highlands, Buttermilk, and Snowmass — each with a distinct character. Here's how to make the most of all of them.

01
Ski Aspen Mountain (Ajax) — The Classic
The mountain that looms directly above downtown — steep, challenging, and the most scenic ski experience in Colorado. Ride the Silver Queen Gondola from the base of town, ski to your hotel at the bottom. This is what Aspen is famous for.
⛷️ Expert Terrain
02
Ski Aspen Highlands — The Powder Days
The local's mountain — Highland Bowl requires a hike but delivers the best ungroomed terrain in the state. Limelight Lounge at the base serves excellent food. Come here when there's been new snow overnight.
⛷️ Advanced / Expert
03
Après-Ski at Ajax Tavern or the J-Bar
The essential Aspen ritual — boots off, hot drink in hand, somewhere warm and lively while the mountain turns golden. Ajax Tavern for outdoor sun-soaked après at the base; the J-Bar for Victorian warmth downtown. The order matters less than the participation.
🥂 Daily Ritual
04
Snowmass — For Families and Vertigo
Aspen's largest mountain — 3,332 acres, the most beginner and intermediate terrain, and a village at the base with its own dining and lodging. The Elk Camp Meadows beginner area is the best place to learn to ski in the state.
⛷️ All Levels
05
Ice Skating at Silver Circle
An outdoor rink at the base of Aspen Mountain — open evenings and weekends, with the town lights and mountain silhouette above you. One of the most beautiful settings for ice skating anywhere in the country.
⛸️ Evening Activity
06
Snowshoeing the Maroon Bells
The most photographed mountain view in Colorado — two 14,000-foot peaks reflected in Maroon Lake. In winter, snowshoe tours make the valley accessible even without skis. One of the great American wilderness experiences, 12 miles from downtown Aspen.
🏔️ Guided Tours Available
Getting There

Arriving in the mountains

Getting to Aspen is part of the adventure. The airport is spectacular but weather-dependent; the drive from Denver is beautiful but long. Plan your arrival carefully.

✈️
Aspen/Pitkin County Airport (ASE)
Served by United (Denver, Los Angeles), American (Chicago, Dallas, Los Angeles, New York), and Delta (Atlanta, Los Angeles). The approach into ASE is among the most dramatic in commercial aviation — runway ends at a cliff. Weather cancellations are real; build buffer days into your trip.
Always have a backup plan. Weather delays at ASE during peak ski season are common. Consider travel insurance for multi-day skiing trips.
✈️
Eagle County (EGE) — Backup Option
Vail's airport — 70 miles from Aspen, about 90 minutes by car or shuttle. More flight options and fewer weather issues than ASE. Worth considering when ASE fares are prohibitive or flights are sold out during peak weeks.
The drive from EGE through the Roaring Fork Valley is genuinely beautiful — not a hardship.
🚗
Drive from Denver
About 4 hours via I-70 and Highway 82 through Glenwood Canyon — one of the most dramatic drives in America. Requires a 4WD vehicle with snow tires in winter. Highway 82 over Independence Pass closes in winter; the canyon route is the only option.
Leave Denver by noon at the latest on Friday ski weekends — traffic on I-70 after 2pm is brutal.
🚌
Roaring Fork Transportation
The RFTA bus connects Aspen to Glenwood Springs and all points in between — free within Aspen. The town itself is entirely walkable during ski season; you genuinely don't need a car if you're staying in downtown Aspen.
All four ski areas offer free shuttle service from downtown Aspen — no car required to reach any mountain.

Advisor's Aspen tips
  • Book flights into ASE as early as possible — peak week seats (Christmas, Presidents' Day) sell out months ahead.
  • Build a weather buffer day at the start of your trip if flying ASE — closure risk is real.
  • Multi-mountain IKON Pass covers all four Aspen/Snowmass mountains — the best value if you're skiing 4+ days.
  • Element 47 and Matsuhisa reservations fill weeks in advance during peak season. Book the day your trip is confirmed.
  • Altitude hits hard at 8,000 feet — plan a light first day, hydrate aggressively, and avoid alcohol the first evening if you can manage it.
  • The shoulder weeks in late January and early March offer the best combination of snow, crowd, and price.
Best times to visit
  • Late January — Best snow, lowest crowds between holidays
  • Early March — Stable weather, excellent skiing, spring light
  • Food & Wine Classic (June) — The most glamorous summer weekend in America
  • Avoid: Christmas week and Presidents' Day — peak prices and crowds
