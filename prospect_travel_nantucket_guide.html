Where to Stay

Stays worth savoring

Nantucket's best hotels book out months in advance — especially in July and August. These are the properties I return to again and again, each with a distinct character that makes the island feel like your own.

Luxury · Top Pick
White Elephant Hotel
Nantucket's grande dame — a waterfront landmark with harbor views, a full-service spa, and dining that rivals anything on island. The cottages are perfection for families.

Relais & Châteaux · Most Romantic
The Wauwinet
At the far eastern tip of the island, Nantucket's only Relais & Châteaux property offers private ocean and bayside beaches, a legendary restaurant, and complete seclusion from the summer crowd.

A beautifully restored sea captain's home steps from Main Street — Portuguese tiles, antique furnishings, and one of the island's best cocktail bars. Sophisticated, intimate, and impeccably run.

Restaurants & Dining

Tables worth the wait

Nantucket punches well above its weight for a small island — driven by the quality of what comes off the boats each morning and a dining scene that has quietly become one of the best in New England.

Topper's at The Wauwinet
$$$$
Wauwinet · East of Town
The island's most celebrated fine dining — prix-fixe and à la carte in an elegant setting overlooking the harbor. Arrive for the lawn cocktails, stay for the tasting menu. Book months in advance.
→ Order: Topper's clam chowder, whatever the day boat caught
CRU Oyster Bar
$$$
Straight Wharf · Waterfront
The hardest reservation on the island — a French-Mediterranean raw bar and restaurant perched at the end of the wharf. The lobster roll is the best on island. Sells out for the entire month within a day of opening reservations.
→ Order: Oysters, the CRU lobster roll, anything with the catch of the day

Things to Do

The island, properly

Nantucket rewards those who slow down. There's no rush here — the island runs on its own clock, and the best experiences come from knowing where to be at the right moment.

01
Walk Cobblestone Main Street at Dawn
Before the summer crowd arrives, Nantucket's historic downtown is utterly peaceful. The whaling-era architecture, the antique shops still shuttered, the scent of coffee from the bakeries opening — it's the island at its most authentic. Worth an early alarm.
🌅 Morning Experience
02
Rent Bikes to 'Sconset
The 8-mile bike path from town to the village of Siasconset — rose-covered cottages, a bluff with Atlantic views, and a tiny market for sandwiches — is the quintessential Nantucket afternoon. Go early, have lunch, cycle back in time for sunset.
🚲 Half-Day Adventure
03
Sail from Nantucket Town
Charter a sailboat for a sunset cruise around the harbor — or a full-day trip to the outer islands. Several excellent operators run both private and group charters. This is why people come to Nantucket. Get on the water.
⛵ Charter Available
04
Nantucket Whaling Museum
One of the finest maritime museums in America, housed in a former spermaceti candle factory. The 46-foot sperm whale skeleton is extraordinary. Genuinely fascinating for adults and children alike — allow two hours.
🐋 Cultural Experience
05
Madaket Beach at Sunset
The far western tip of the island, where the sun drops into the water — one of the few places on the East Coast with a true western-facing sunset over the ocean. Bring a drink, a blanket, and nothing else. Arrive 30 minutes early.
🌅 Sunset Ritual
06
Great Point Lighthouse by 4WD
The undeveloped northern tip of the island — accessible only by 4WD over sand — with a lighthouse, seals in the water, and complete solitude. Rent a permit-equipped Jeep and make a morning of it. One of the most beautiful spots in New England.
🚙 4WD Required
Getting There

Reaching the island

Nantucket is 30 miles offshore — and the journey is part of the experience. There are two good ways in: ferry from Hyannis or direct flight. Both have their advocates.

⛴️
High-Speed Ferry
The Steamship Authority and Hy-Line Cruises both run high-speed passenger ferries from Hyannis, MA — about 1 hour each way. Book in advance for peak summer departures. The slow car ferry (2.25 hrs) is how you bring a vehicle.
High-speed ferry is the classic Nantucket arrival. There's nothing like pulling into the harbor for the first time.
✈️
Direct Flights
Cape Air, JetBlue, and American connect Nantucket (ACK) to Boston, New York, and Philadelphia. Cape Air's small props from Boston take 35 minutes. Summer routes fill fast — book well ahead.
Flying in gives you spectacular aerial views of the island on approach. Worth it at least once.
🚗
Getting Around on Island
You don't need a car for a town-based stay — the island is entirely walkable or bikeable. If you're venturing to the outer beaches or Great Point, you'll want a 4WD vehicle rental. Taxis and rideshares are limited; plan ahead.
The NRTA shuttle covers most of the island for $2 a ride — a secret weapon in peak season.
🚌
Car Ferry (Vehicles)
The slow Steamship Authority ferry from Hyannis takes cars (2.25 hrs). Reservations open months in advance and are essential in summer — missing your slot means missing the day. Book the moment reservations open.
Honestly? Leave the car in Hyannis. Bikes and taxis are more than enough for most Nantucket trips.

Advisor's Nantucket tips
  • Book ferry reservations the moment they open — Steamship Authority summer slots vanish within hours.
  • CRU reservations open for a full month at once. Set a reminder and be online at the stroke of midnight.
  • Bring a light rain jacket — the island creates its own weather and afternoon fog can roll in fast.
  • Rent bikes from Young's Bicycle Shop on Broad Street — they've been doing it since 1931 and know the island cold.
  • The shoulder season (late May and early October) offers the best combination of lower crowds and lovely weather.
  • Don't try to drive everywhere — the island is meant to be walked and biked. Leave the car mentality at home.
Best times to visit
  • Late June — Long days, warm water, before peak crowds arrive
  • Early September — Summer lingers, crowds thin, restaurants still fully open
  • Christmas Stroll (December) — The island's most magical weekend of the year
  • Avoid: July 4th week and last two weeks of July — peak crowds and prices
